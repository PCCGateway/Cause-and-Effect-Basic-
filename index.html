1. Plan your essay

A cause explains why something happens. An effect explains what happens because of it. Choose one clear direction for your essay.
Thesis frame: [Topic] has three important [causes/effects]: [point 1], [point 2], and [point 3].

2. Introduction

Your introduction should have a hook → background → thesis. Avoid starting with “In this essay I will...”

Your thesis from Step 1 is automatically added at the end of this paragraph.

3. Body paragraph 1

Structure: topic sentence → explanation → evidence → analysis → closing/transition. Explain how the evidence supports your point; do not just drop in a quotation.

4. Body paragraph 2

Transition ideas: In addition, Another important cause, As a result, Similarly, Furthermore. Connect this paragraph to your previous point.

5. Body paragraph 3

Transition ideas: Finally, A third significant effect, Equally important, Over time. Keep the focus on your third main point.

6. Conclusion

Do not introduce brand-new evidence. Restate the thesis differently → revisit the three points → explain why it matters → end strongly.

7. Two outside sources & MLA Works Cited

Use two credible outside sources that support your topic. Paste the URL and enter the source information. A URL by itself is usually not enough for a correct MLA citation, and this tool cannot reliably identify authors and dates from every website.

MLA webpage pattern: Last Name, First Name. “Page Title.” Website Name, Publisher (if different), Day Month Year, URL. Omit unavailable elements. Check author, title, date, and website against the original page.

Your in-text citations in body paragraphs should match the first element of the corresponding Works Cited entry.

8. Review and download

Before submitting, check: five paragraphs, clear thesis, three topic sentences, logical transitions, evidence explained in your own words, two credible sources, matching in-text citations, and a Works Cited page.
